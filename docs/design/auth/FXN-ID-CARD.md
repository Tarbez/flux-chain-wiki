# The FXN ID card on defxn.com

Layer: app. The card is a page feature of the site. It draws with fxn-3d's web player, it reads only what the sign-in kit
shows the person, and it writes nothing. The kernel, the capabilities and the resolvers do not change.

Status: built, not confirmed by the owner (2026-10-31). No tests lock it yet. Tests come after the owner confirms the design.

## What it is

The FXN ID card is the 3D card FXN Browser shows. It plays the fxn-3d document `fxnbrowseridcardassembly` (idcard01, turned
wide) through fxn-3d's embed player. No Three.js is involved. The site shows it in two places:

- **/auth.** The card replaced the world's own card, and stands where that card stood. It is blank (frame 0: the plain
  card, no plate on it) until you press "Create a new identity". Then it builds piece by piece at the build's own pace,
  and does each step's part. Your name types onto it, the PIN seats the security plate, and the password
  seals it. The key word and the icon strike the stamp. The word count turns the card over to its recovery vault. The
  Emergency Kit lights the vault's cells once you confirm you wrote the words down. The saved file turns it back finished. Back to sign in plays it back to blank. With an FXN ID open on the tab
  it stands finished at its welcome pose, with your name.
- **/account, once your FXN ID file is open.** The card stands at its Master ID pose under your identity details and
  leans toward the pointer. "Open your FXN ID" opens the FXN ID view. The card opens, and your Public IDs stand inside it
  as layers. Each layer holds its wallets. The view explains itself in four beats the first time (Master ID, Public IDs,
  wallets, where to manage them). "How it works" replays the beats. The first time this device sees an open file on
  /account, the view opens by itself, the way FXN Browser's does after a sign-in.

The card follows the theme. Its painted accent turns to the theme's `--primary` by a CSS hue-rotate. The prints on it take
`--primary` directly. The view uses the theme's surfaces and text, in light and dark. A theme change while the card shows
retints it at once.

## One input: the create panel's onCard

`js/ark/auth-kit-create-panel.js` tells the card each step through `opts.onCard(frame, info)`, with
`info = { screen, name, pinGrade, passwordGrade, icon, total, picked }`. These are grades and counts only, never a PIN, a
password, a key word or a recovery word. /auth's wizard is not that panel, but it builds the same `info` and calls the same
`onCard` (`fxn-id-card-stage.js`). Its PIN and password grades come from the panel's one rule
(`ArkAuthKitCreatePanel.grades`), so /auth now loads the panel script for that.

| /auth step | `screen` | Card state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | `name` | name |
| PIN | `pin` | pin |
| Password | `password` | password |
| Key word, Icon | `word` | word (the stamp) |
| Recovery words, and while the file is made | `words` | words (the vault) |
| Emergency Kit, after "I wrote down the phrase" | `confirm` | confirm (every cell lit) |

## The world's old card is gone from /auth

`js/world2/world2-host.mjs` has a table, `NO_MESH_CARD`, of views that draw no mesh card. `auth` is in it. The host never
fetches that view's mesh (no `card.flx`, no blob, no textures). With no card there is no card frame, so the parts written
in the card's own units (the rig's glow and the loose bits off its corner) are not drawn either. The stage's rings, cubes and
glass status panels stay. `world/card.flx` holds a mesh for `auth` only, so no other site page drew that card. The dev
viewers `world/card.html` and `world/look-test.html` still read it directly, and `tests/world-grammar.cjs` still reads its
records. Neither the records nor the encoder changed.

## Blank first

`'blank'` names frame 0 of the timeline, the plain card before its build lays a plate (`fxn-id-card.js` `frameOf`). The
stage (`fxn-id-card-stage.js`) shows it until a flow starts. The first step after blank runs the build to welcome at the
build's own pace, and only then goes on to the step, so the build is always seen whole. A blank card shows no still picture
while the player loads, because the still is the finished card.

## The card's visual rules

The owner judged the card's look better than the world's 3D illustration (2026-10-31). These are the rules that make the card look
better. The world now reads them from one file, `js/world2/world2-cardlook.mjs`.

1. **Satin metal under a clear coat.** The card's plates are metal (metalness about .35, roughness about .4) under a tight clear
   coat. Their bevels catch the key light as a thin bright line, and the plates reflect the room. Nothing is flat matte, except a
   surface that carries words.
2. **One key light, crisp.** One key from high left makes clear shadows and bright edges, and a strong back light outlines
   each piece. Haze stays far back, so near pieces keep their contrast. There is no surface grain to muddy them.
3. **Colour is light, not paint.** The metal stays neutral. The one accent is the theme's base hue (`--primary`). It appears
   only as light: glow strips, lit cells, the label and its rule. A kind's own colour is a clean tint on bright metal, never a
   dark mud.
4. **Layers you can count.** Each plate sits on its own depth with a gap and a shadow under it, so depth reads at a glance.
5. **Type from the site's slots.** Labels use the site's mono face, upper case, tracked .14em, in the accent, with a short rule
   under them. Names use the strong ink. Every size sits on the type scale (11, 18, 29). Words are painted at screen
   resolution, so they stay crisp.
6. **Theme by tokens.** Ink, ground and accent come from theme tokens. A theme change repaints at once, in light and dark.
7. **Motion eases and settles.** Every move uses the same curves (`cubic-bezier(.22, .7, .18, 1)`, in-out cubic, a small
   overshoot on arrival). A move plays back the way it came. Nothing moves without a cause.

### What the world took from them (2026-10-31)

- **Materials** (`world2-cardlook.mjs`): the chain views' record tiles and the identity blocks moved from flat matte (metal .08,
  rough .6) to the card's satin. Links, your key, the auth stage's cubes, the status panels, the glass panes and the black shell
  each take a card material. The Kit and the Sites keep the materials their own records carry.
- **Light and air** (`js/content/world-data.js`, re-encoded into `world/world.flx` by `scripts/world-encode.mjs`): key 2.3 to
  2.6, shadow softness 9 to 6, back light 1.05 to 1.35, room 0.16 to 0.24, contrast 0.32 to 0.42, grain 1 to 0.35, dust 150 to
  80, and haze starting at 1.1 (was 0.93) and full at 3 (was 2.4) times the view distance. Only these knob records changed in
  `world.flx`.
- **Type:** the status panels and record plates now use the site's font slots. They named JetBrains Mono and Inter Tight,
  which the site does not load, so they had fallen back to system faces. The panels paint at 1024 wide (was 512), with the
  card's label for the title, and take the theme's ink, so they read in a light theme. The world paints its words again once
  the fonts have loaded.
- **Labels on the 3D** (`css/world.css` `.world-tags`): the card's label. The title is mono, 600 weight, tracked .14em, in
  `--primary`, and every size is 11 (they were 12, 10 and 9).
- **Palette** (`css/world.css`): each chain kind and network keeps its hue. Each tone is lifted to at least 46% lightness, and
  muddy saturations are cleaned up.

### The engine's part (fxn-3d, 2026-10-31, approved by the owner for this work)

The rings, links and cubes of the network are drawn by fxn-3d's lit program, which was Lambert only. Two files in
`fxn-3d/engine/render` now take a surface:

- `world-shaders.mjs`, the lit program: a part with a material gets the card's surface (`shineOf`). That is a GGX highlight
  of the key and back light, a tight clear coat, the room as a sky-to-ground reflection weighted by Fresnel, and metal
  that darkens the diffuse and tints the highlight. It also gets a bevel line in the part's own colour, a third of the way
  to the theme's base hue, so every edge shares the accent and a kind keeps its colour. The network's glass tiles take it
  too. A link with `flow` carries a pulse of the theme's hue along its length. Dotted lines carry the same pulse.
- `world-shaders.mjs`, the floor: fine lines every half unit that fade out before they would crowd a pixel, a soft sheen
  round the network, and a shadow catcher with its own strength and a wider 4x4 spread. The catcher's strength drops
  further in a light theme, which removes the blotchy ring shadows. The slots' light discs also fade in a light theme.
- `world.mjs`: a part's material is `p.mat = { rough, coat, metal, edge, flow }`. The new look knobs are `floorfine`,
  `floorsheen`, `shadowstrength`, `shadowsoft`, `shadowlight`, `flow`, `flowspeed` and `slotlight`. A part with no
  material, and a look that names none of the knobs, draw exactly as before.

The site gives the frame's parts their materials from one table (`world2-cardlook.mjs` `FRAME`, applied once per part by
`world2-host.mjs`). The knob values live in `js/content/world-data.js`, with their schema in `js/content/world.js`. The
light along the links runs from each view's arrival for 9 seconds, eases out, and then the world stops drawing. With
reduced motion it does not run. Behind the canvas, `css/world.css` adds two layers of air from the theme: a wash of the
base hue where the network stands, and a horizon band the far floor fades into.

### Not built

- Home's camera is baked into the encoder (`HOME_DIST`, `HOME_ELEV`), and every ring is cast through it. The per-route

- No screen-space reflection pass: the floor's mirror is a second draw of the lit solids. The mesh-pass pieces (the chain

- The mesh pass (`engine/render/program.mjs`) is shared with the studio and the card player, and is left as it was. The
  pieces get the card's look from their geometry and materials, not from a new shader.

## Merging with FXN Browser

The site's files are the browser's files in the same shape (`ark-browser src/renderer/fxn-id-card/`). The two hosts differ
in three named places, so the files can become one shared module:

1. **PATHS** at the top of `fxn-id-card.js`. These say where the player, the pack, the still and the sheet live, and which
   `<html>` attributes a theme change writes. A shared module takes these from its host.
2. **HOST** at the top of `fxn-id-card.css`. Every rule after it reads only `--fxn-*` names. The browser's tokens are whole
   colours (`var(--accent)`), while the site's are HSL triplets (`hsl(var(--primary))`) on its own `--fs-*` type scale.
   The browser's `--accent` and the site's `--primary` play the same part: the base hue.
3. **The glue.** The browser keeps `showFxnIdCard` inside `renderer.js`. The site keeps it in `fxn-id-card-stage.js`. The
   shared module should carry the stage file, and the browser's renderer should call it.

The site made three changes inside the shared code. Each is marked "site:" in the code. The browser can take both as they are:

- `attach()` waits for the sheet before the first tint, because the tint reads `--fxn-idc-accent`, which the sheet
  defines.
- `'blank'` is a state (frame 0), and a blank card does not show the finished still while it loads.
- The view's layers and wallet coins use the theme's glass, not fixed dark ink, so they follow light and dark.

The pack ships as base64 in a module (`card-pack.js`, 1.3 MB) because the browser's CSP cannot fetch it. The site could
fetch the binary pack instead (about 0.97 MB) with `connect-src 'self'`. The site keeps the same file for now, so both hosts
load the pack the same way.

## Reduction test

**/auth (the card in the stage):**

1. About: making your FXN ID, one step at a time.
2. Dominant action: "Create a new identity" while the card is blank, then the wizard's own (Next, then "Create my FXN ID",
   then "Save FXN ID file"). The card adds no action.
3. Know now: which step you are on, and that it has changed the card.
4. Hidden until asked: nothing new. The card shows grades and counts, never a secret.
5. Repeated elsewhere: the step rail names the step too. The card shows it rather than saying it again.
6. Only because the data has it: the name, the grades, the icon and the word count, as typed.
7. Removable: the whole card. The wizard works without it, and a failed load leaves the steps alone.
   The world's old card was removed: two cards said the same thing.
8. Leaving keeps state: leaving /auth stops the player and frees its WebGL context.
9. Returning: the card is blank again, and "Create a new identity" builds it again from the start.
10. Without WebGL2: the settled still picture stands in, with its prints.

**/account, your FXN ID (the small card and the view):**

1. About: what your FXN ID holds. That is one Master ID, its Public IDs and their wallets.
2. Dominant action: in the view, the guide's Next while the guide shows. After the guide, "Save the file" once something
   has changed. Save takes the complement only while there is a change to save.
3. Know now: which Public ID sites see, and which wallets are published.
4. Hidden until asked: full keys and dates (the detail panel shows them for the item in focus).
5. Repeated elsewhere: Signing keys lists the same Public IDs. The view is the picture of them, and Signing keys is the
   list. Closing the view refreshes the list.
6. Only because the data has it: a "Published" mark, the "Sites see this one" badge, a wallet count.
7. Removable: the small card on /account. The button alone would still open the view.
8. Leaving keeps state: changes live in the open file until you save it. The view says so after each change.
9. Returning: the guide does not replay (this device keeps one mark, `defxn-fxn-id-view-explained`, in localStorage).
10. Without WebGL2 or with reduced motion: the view draws the same tree in 2D.
