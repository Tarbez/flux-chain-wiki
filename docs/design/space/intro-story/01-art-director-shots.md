# Intro film: shots and composition (art director, 2026-11-09)

Layer: app (the site's intro page over the fxn-3d objects). Reader: the producer who merges the story, then the site session that builds it. Sources: SPACE.md (last two sections), intro.js, underground/NOTES.md round 5, OBJECT-QUEUE.md, worldidentity.js, chain.js, 00-facts-genesis.md. Nothing in the film is live, and one plate on screen says so (shot 3).

## The visual rule

- One continuous camera move. Cuts land only on an object's lock. No fade, fog or blur. Depth is stone in shadow against lit strips (the Portal 101 look).
- Three distances, one job each. **Wide** (24 mm) says where you are. **Medium** (35 mm) says what the object does. **Close** (50 to 85 mm) says what the key does. Nothing but the key gets a close shot.
- Light is the story. The hall starts dark, and each object earns its light by acting. Mint is the record and the network. Amber is the visitor's act: a press, a signature, a grant. At most three amber points burn at once.
- The camera stays at eye height, a person walking a hall of 4 to 9 m objects. It looks up only in the screen room.
- Words come after the act, beside the object (intro.js cine).

## The identity motif: the key that never leaves the frame

The visitor's identity is the crystal key from Name Gate (`key-gate/reference.png`). The site already calls it that: "A key file on your device that signs you into every network" (worldidentity.js).

1. **Made once, on screen.** It forms in the glitch dive (shot 2) from the shards of the torn portal picture. Nobody hands it over.
2. **Parked, never lost.** From shot 3 on it hovers low-left in the frame, inside the 3D, 1.2 m off the floor, turning slowly, through every cut. It never travels to an object.
3. **It signs by touch, and only a line leaves.** A record that needs signing comes to the key. The key tips, its /// lights amber for one beat, and a thin amber line runs into the record. The line goes and the key stays: "Your key never leaves your device. The server checks your signature. Never holds the key."
4. **Granting access is a pane, not a hand-over.** When a site asks (shot 10), a card-sized mint pane opens between the key and the asker, showing the exact site and the exact bytes. The key tips amber: Share. The pane crosses and dims when the asker leaves ("A yes lasts for that tab only"). Don't share: the pane cracks and drops. The key does not move. Never a copy of the key.
5. **Your chain is your own.** Each signed record stacks under the key as a short lit spine ("Every identity signs its own chain", chain.js). By shot 14 it holds seven records, none erased.
6. **Keeping it safe is three beats** (shot 11): a case seals (PIN), a five-slot gate lights three and releases (3 of 5), a second key forms from the first (New key).
