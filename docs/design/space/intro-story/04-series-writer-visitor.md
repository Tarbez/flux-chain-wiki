# 04 · The film from inside the visitor

Layer: app (the site intro, `js/pages/intro.js`, over the objects from fxn-3d). Reader: the producer who merges the nine drafts, then the
object builders. Status: draft, not confirmed. Nothing here is built. Facts: `00-facts-genesis.md`, `00-facts-browser.md`. The devnet
is not deployed and nothing is publicly released, so the film shows **what the visitor will hold**, as a rehearsal. The 11 mono line
`A REHEARSAL. NOTHING IS LIVE.` stands under the screen from beat 2 to beat 10.

## Who arrives

A person on a phone or a laptop. Their photos sit on one company's servers, their messages on another's, their money in a bank app, their
handle on a platform that can close it. They have pressed "I agree" a hundred times and read it never. They call this how things are.

Someone sent the link, or they heard "a new internet" and expect a pitch, or they want to build something. Their first feeling is
**guard**. The film's job: turn "how things are" into "that is mine", without saying so.

## Three candidate "this is me" moments

**A. The key takes form (beat 3).** Parts travel in on rails and lock: a seal, a lock ring, a tag. It drops to the desk and glows. Nobody
handed it over. It was made in front of them, in their room. The line: "Made on your device. It never leaves it. The server checks your
signature and never holds the key." True in the dev build (browser facts §1), shown as what they will hold. Weakness: a key is a cold thing
to love.

**B. The chain grows from the key (beat 4, extended).** The key signs. The first record stands up beside it, then the second, each linked to
the one before. The line: "Every record you sign joins your own chain. Nothing in it is rewritten." Your identity is its own chain (the
`/identity` and `/chain` pages). Their history becomes a thing they can hold and turn. Weakness: it costs about 4 s of the budget.

**C. The name on the door (beat 7).** A name tag forms on the archive door, blank. A name is the most human mark. Weakness: a `.fxn` name is
built, not released, and claims stay genesis-key-only until the public access gate exists. If shown, the door stays blank and the line says
"Names open after genesis." Never show a name as claimed.

My pick: **A, with B's first two records.** They carry the key. The chain is why it matters.

## The choice: who may see what (beat 7)

The big screen shows three records from their chain, drawn as closed envelopes on the desk. Beside each, the two words from `/identity`:
**Share** and **Don't share**. Three asking lights appear at the edge of the room, one per envelope, each a plain shape: a shop, a tool, an
unknown node. No names, no logos. They are sites asking to sign you in, never people: sharing with another person is built but off behind a
flag (browser facts §2), so it stays out.

- Hover an asking light: the envelope it wants lifts a little. Its label says what would be seen: "This record. Not the key."
- Press **Share**: the envelope opens toward that light only. A thin line runs out. A receipt returns. The line: "You see the exact site
  and the exact bytes. Then you sign, on your device."
- Press **Don't share**: the envelope stays shut. The light dims and goes out. Nothing is written.
- Press a lit line a second time: it cuts. The light goes out. The line: "You can take it back." Grants are stored and can be revoked (§2).
- Do nothing for two bars: the service lamp pulses once. The chip nudges 2 px. The film waits.

The key never moves. The server shape checks a signature and never holds the key.

The choice carries: shared envelopes keep a faint line to their light through beats 8-10. Withheld ones stay dark and sealed.

## Carried, and shown only on their terms

1. The key stays in frame from beat 3 to beat 10. At Chain Drum or Bridge Span it is a point of light at the edge. The same key signs
   each thing. Carrying it across machines and worlds depends on the chain and is not released (§3): the film shows the design, and the
   words claim no more.
2. The receipts collect in a stack beside the key. By beat 10 the stack is the visitor's history of this visit, and the door reads it.
3. The withheld envelopes are still there at the gate. The light through Release Gate passes over them and they stay sealed. What they
   kept, they still have.

## The feeling they leave with

Not "I understand FXN." That is the site's job after the gate. The target is one sentence they could say unprompted:
**"That room was mine, and I chose what left it."**

"Cool graphics" means it failed. "So my stuff would be in there and I pick who sees it" means it worked.

## Hard rules I kept

- Welcome first. Nothing sells before beat 2. Show, then name: no line before its object has acted.
- Nothing planned shown as live. Live Room stays out: streaming is design only (§6). Reward Flow stays out: nothing has paid out (§3). No
  visitor earns money in the film. Names stay blank. No per-site keys.
- A world is not a chain (§7). World Forge, if built, shows a scene being built, never a chain being founded.
- Plain words. No lore names, no religion. The lore's dark-then-one-lamp look is used, its places and people stay out.
- One dominant action per beat, in the complement, once.
