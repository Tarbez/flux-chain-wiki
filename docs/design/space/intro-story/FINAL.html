The intro film: one true story, in four acts.

Written by a team of ten: two art directors, two series writers, a product designer, two storytellers, a story editor, a copywriter and a producer. Every line is checked against the facts that four other sessions sent in. No metaphors. Nothing is built yet.

128 s of film4 acts21 shots1 choice for the visitor3 new objects5 decisions for you

The story in one sentence

A visitor walks through the portal into their archive, initiates an FXN ID on their device, signs a record that joins the ID's own chain and comes back with a final receipt copied to our nodes, decides Share or Don't share when a site asks, keeps the key when a site asks for it, opens the same file on a second device, and then sees that key start a chain, ship an app and open the gate to the game, with every claim carrying its status.

What every visitor knows at the end

  1. This room is my archive. Records I sign land here, and the network under it keeps them in copies.
  2. I initiated my FXN ID on my device. It is a key file, and no server holds it. I name it and seal it with a PIN.
  3. Each record I sign joins my own chain. A receipt comes back, and that receipt is final. Nothing is rewritten.
  4. When a site asks, I see the exact site and bytes, then I say Share or Don't share. A site gets my signature, never my key.
  5. The same key starts a chain, ships an app and opens the gate. Keepers hold the copies. The pay rule has paid nobody yet. This runs on our development network today. Genesis is next.

The identity, in five acts

By the gate, the card's rail is the visitor's own chain. That is a fact: every identity signs its own chain (built, not live).

Initiate FXN ID · shot 5

Shown: the card builds, the key locks on it, nothing leaves the frame.

True today: the key file is initiated at /auth and never uploaded. That gate is live on the old system.

Shape · shot 6

Shown: a name, a PIN and an icon go onto the card.

True today: the live wizard. .fxn names open after genesis. A new key and trustee recovery are planned.

Carry · shots 7 to 17

Shown: the same key signs a record, a chain launch and a bundle, and the same file opens on a phone.

True today: the file opens on another device at /auth. Carrying it across worlds is built, not released.

Grant · shot 10

Shown: a site asks, the pane shows the exact site and bytes, Share or Don't share.

True today: a yes lasts for that tab only on the web. Stored grants with revoke exist in the browser's dev build.

Refuse · shot 11

Shown: a frame asks for the key itself. The key does not move. A signature goes instead.

True today: the key never leaves the device. The server checks a signature and never holds the key.

The end action

On screen: "Your key is an FXN ID." and the button Initiate FXN ID, plus "I have one. Sign in."

Why it is honest: it opens the live /auth flow. Never "Download", "Play now" or "Earn".

Controls and rules

Always on screen

  • Skip (Esc), Pause and Play (Space)
  • Four act dots (keys 1 to 4)
  • "Why?": one panel with the reason and every number with its conditions
  • The object's name and line, after it acts

Light means status

  • Filled mint: live now
  • Edge light: built, not live
  • Dark outline: design only
  • Amber is only the visitor's act: the key, a press, a signature

Rhythm

  • One object per 8 s cell: build 4 s, words 2 s, rest 2 s
  • Parts build in steps on rails and lock
  • Five big moments, each followed by a hold

Type

  • FXN Basalt at 76, 47 and 29 px
  • IBM Plex Mono at 11 px for labels and conditions
  • Words never fade. A number never stands without its condition.

Objects

Used as they are

Every site object except Name Gate (parked, its key part reused), plus the archive.

Cut, with the reason

Reward Flow (nothing has paid out). World Forge (a world is not a chain). Record Vault and Live Room (the screen draws them as plans).

Small changes

Kernel screen draws the two plans. Keeper Tower gets a four-step gauge. Rule Book gets the pay page. Chain Drum starts a floor spine. Network Wall fills four tiles. Ledger Line returns the receipt to the card.

New objects, built one at a time

  1. FXN ID card: a card plate, the key on its edge, a seal, the name and icon faces, its own rail for signed records, and a phone plate.
  2. Access Pane: a card-sized mint pane with the site and the bytes, a crack-and-drop state, and a key-shaped slot the key does not fit.
  3. Keeper Array: sixteen racks from Keeper Tower's parts, the last one desk-sized (your own machine), plus the gauge plate.
