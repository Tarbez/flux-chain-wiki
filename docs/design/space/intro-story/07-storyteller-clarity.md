## 3. What the film must carry, and what waits

The film has 60 seconds. A visitor holds five ideas, not eighteen. These five are the film:

1. **This is your archive.** Every record you sign lands here, in copies.
2. **Your key is yours.** It lives on your device. A site asks, and you decide, tab by tab.
3. **A record goes in, a receipt comes back.** The receipt is the proof. Nothing is rewritten.
4. **Keepers hold the copies.** Anyone's machine can be one. More keepers, more room.
5. **You can make things.** An app, a chain, a world. The gate to the game opens with your key.

Everything else is an image the camera passes, with its true line and a Why button. A part that is not live shows dark, labelled "after genesis". A lit screen for a design-only feature is a lie the visitor finds later.

## 4. Words a first visitor does not know

The film says the plain word first: **record** ("what you did, written down"), **keeper** ("a machine that holds copies"), **chain** ("a list of records in order"), **hash** (say "checked"). **Resolver**, **grammar**, **multisig**, **devnet**, **DAO** and **stake** never appear in the film. "The kernel puts every record in order" is the only kernel line a visitor needs.

## 5. Lines the film must not say

"Worlds are chains" (no file supports it). "You earn by running a node" (nothing has paid out). "Thousands of nodes" (three named pinners). Any final/s number in the film's voice. "Stream, play, collaborate" (design only). "It runs today" without naming the network.
