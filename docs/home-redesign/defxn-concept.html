For the designer · concept brief

DEFXN. How it works, and what it makes.

This page explains DEFXN to a designer. It covers the idea, the parts, the products and the rules every screen follows. Read sections 1 to 3 first. They hold the whole idea.

Written23 October 2026
Network stateDevnet only. Nothing is public yet.
Rule for this pageEvery number names its source

01

In one minute

DEFXN is a network where every result is signed. A signed result can be checked by anyone, at any time, with no trust in a company.

  1. Every identity has its own chain. A person, a team or an app keeps its own history of signed records.
  2. Every token keeps its own ledger. Each token has its own history of who held how much.
  3. Resolvers do the work and sign the result. A resolver is a rulebook. It reads signed records, applies its rules, and signs what follows from them. That signature is the result.
  4. Other computers keep a copy. They store the signed result as it is. They do not redo the work.
  5. Anyone can check. A person who wants proof follows the signatures back to the start.

FXN is the first chain, and its coin is FXN. Builders launch their own resolvers. Computers that run resolvers or store data earn from that work. DAOs govern each network with public rules.

The one sentence for the home page today: "Every identity has its own chain. Every token keeps its own ledger. Resolvers do the work and sign the result. Other computers keep a copy."

03

How a result is made

This is the core of DEFXN. It has five steps. There is no sixth step. The example is a swap: two people trade one token for another.

  1. Two parties sign their intent. Each intent is a record. Its last field is the signature.
  2. A resolver resolves. It checks every signature in one batch, refuses replays, and clears all swaps in the batch at one price. Your place in the batch does not change your price.
  3. The resolver signs the result. That signature is the result. Nothing has to happen later to make it true.
  4. Other computers store the signed result. They do not check it again and do not redo it. Storing is their whole job.
  5. Anyone can check it. They follow the signatures back. For a swap this takes three signature checks.

Trust is a choice you make before you send. A signature shows who signed. It does not show that the rulebook is good. So each resolver shows three public facts: its grammar, the capabilities it is built from, and who can change it. Screens must show these three facts wherever a person decides to use a resolver.

06

Life of a resolver

This is the builder's path. Products in section 7 cover each step.

  1. Build. The builder picks capabilities and writes the grammar for the records the resolver accepts. Resolver Studio is a canvas of nodes for this. A resolver can also wait on another resolver's result, for example "when the payment is confirmed, give access".
  2. Launch. One signed launch record gives the resolver its own chain. The builder sees the exact record before signing. The network refuses a launch that uses a capability that is not launched.
  3. Choose who can change it. With no control key the resolver can never change. With a control key, only that key can sign an upgrade. An upgrade adds a new version. The old one stays on the line.
  4. Serve. A provider runs it after it passes the checked cases of every capability inside. Until then the resolver is launched but not served.
  5. Inspect. Explore shows every resolver: its grammar, its parts, its providers and its whole history. The page checks every signature itself.
Design note. "Launched" and "served" are two different states. Show them differently: a dashed outline for launched, a solid one with a proof badge for served.

08

Identity and names

FXN ID

FXN ID is a person's identity. It is one key file, .auth.fxn, for every network. The file stays on the person's device. It is never uploaded.

Apps sign in the browser. The server never holds the key. Every app that asks for a signature shows the full request: what will be signed, for which network, and for how long.

A site learns who you are only after you choose "Share". A yes lasts for that tab and that identity only. It is never stored. Every signature still opens the full review, with the exact site and the exact bytes.

The file holds no Secret Key. The Secret Key is a 128-bit key that the FXN ID gate holds in pieces: 5 gate nodes, and any 3 of them release it. The file alone is useless to a thief.

The gate releases the Secret Key only for the right factors: a PIN, an optional password, an optional key word and one of nine icons. The PIN has 6 to 12 digits, or 8 or more with no password. Three wrong tries lock the Secret Key for 24 hours on every gate node.

The Emergency Kit holds the Secret Key and the recovery phrase. It is shown once, when the identity is made, after you type your consent. It opens the file when the gate is down or locked. Keep it offline. Never photograph it or paste it anywhere.

By default, on a page where you open your FXN ID file yourself, the signing key lives only in that tab's memory. A reload drops it. The defxn.com identity window keeps the key in its own tab for every defxn site. One sign-in serves them all, and closing that window signs you out everywhere. The defxn sites remember your name and public key across reloads, never the key itself. A remembered identity cannot sign until you open the key again.

After the file opens, you choose how long this browser keeps your identity: only this tab, or 1, 3, 7 or 14 days. With a time chosen, a reload brings it back without the file. The panel shows the end date and a Forget now button, and Sign out deletes it too. The browser never keeps your PIN, password, key word, icon or Secret Key. Until the time runs out, a harmful script on that site could sign as you. On a shared or public device, choose Only this tab. In the terminal, fxn-id open --remember keeps a session in the macOS Keychain for 1, 3, 7 or 14 days.

Nobody ever needs your PIN, password, key word, icon or Secret Key. That includes support. Only the sign-in box asks for them.

Partial Web sign-in does not ask the gate yet. It opens the file with the Secret Key from your Emergency Kit, typed with your PIN, password, key word and icon. The gate nodes all run on one computer today.

.fxn names

A name is flat: radio.fxn, never radio.defxn.fxn. One system owns every name: the Domain resolver. Other parts only read it and check the proof.

The owner can move a name. The new owner signs too. A name can be sold for FXN.

Partial Until a public access gate exists, only the network's genesis key can claim new names. The first claim wins.

Only a network's genesis key can file the network and mint its coin. A test key is never used on mainnet.

Nodes

A node is a computer that helps run the network. There are three ways to run one.

Browser tab
nodes.defxn.comAny phone or computer browser. No install. The tab checks signatures itself.
Phone app
DEFXN mobileiOS and Android shells around the same node. Runs on test phones.
Server
The fxn downloadOne download, several parts: chain, resolver hosts, relay, store. Each part restarts alone.

A node picks its roles. It can host a chain, run resolvers it has proved, store data, or relay traffic. The chain decides what may run. A node runs nothing that is not on the chain.

FXN and money

These are owner decisions. Most are not in code yet. Show a rule as a rule. Never show a number as live until it is.

Three kinds of money

KindWhat it isCan it move?
Network coinsFXN, DARO, CR3. Each lives on its own native chain.Yes. Trade, send, give.
CreditsMoney to spend inside apps and networks, for features and AI. Bought only with the network's coin.No. Bound to one identity.
App tokensOne per shipped app, named after the app. Each trades against its network's coin.Yes. None is registered yet.

FXN starting supply Decided

1,000,000,000 FXN, fixed on the chain. Three groups:

  • Mining (47%) is paid out on the chain over many years, for measured storage and compute work. The payout gets smaller over time but never reaches zero. Fees then become the main reward.
  • Every other group sits in DAO safes and is released over time. Team: the first eighth at day 90, all by day 720. Operations: monthly over 3 years.
  • The service fund and buyback need time, a DAO vote for each spend, and a cap per 30 days. So nobody, the team included, can move the price with them.
  • Public sale (10%): 0.34 EUR per FXN, in phases over the first year. At most 50,000 FXN per identity. Buys in a phase's first hour fill together. Buyers get 25% at once and the rest monthly over 6 months. Payments go straight to a DAO safe, and every credit names the outside payment so anyone can check it.
The owner's principle: fair by design, not by hope. Every rule that stops abuse is enforced by the chain. Copy shows the rules and the numbers. Copy never says "fair" or "safe" about itself.

Fees Decided

  • The cost of running work: 65% goes to the computers, 35% to the network fund.
  • A resolver owner sets a price. 15% of it pays for builder programs: rewards, contests, grants and bounties. A DAO vote can lower the 15%. Nothing can raise it.
  • No price is set yet. Any price on a screen today must say "Example numbers. The network has not set its prices yet."

All 15,360 swaps cleared at one price per batch. Copies reached all 10 storing computers, and 60 of 60 checks passed.

Swaps
643,632 swaps/s
Transfers
69,895 transfers/s
Stored results
6,044,992 records/s

Limit: no network time is inside these runs. They measure how fast the nodes resolve, not how fast a result reaches you. Sending a result to 10 computers takes 449 ms today.

Rules for numbers on screens

  • A rate names what it counts: swaps/s, transfers/s, records/s. A bare "per second" is wrong.
  • A fleet number is a sum of real runs. It is never one node's number multiplied.
  • "Not measured yet" is different from 0. Use a dash or those words. Never show 0 for something nobody measured.
  • Never invent live activity: no fake tickers, no fake counters, no "Live" badge.

13

Design rules

The owner's laws apply to every screen. The full text is in two files: docs/PRODUCT-DESIGN-LAWS.md (70 laws) and docs/COPY-LAWS.md.

Layout

  • One idea per page. One main action per view.
  • Working screens fit 1280x720 and 1440x900 with no scroll.
  • Other pages are at most 1.6 screens tall, on desktop and on a phone.
  • Depth replaces length: open a thing to see more of it, instead of making the page longer.
  • No marketing inside a product.
  • Phone first. Text at least 16px. Tap targets at least 44px.

Motion

  • Every animation stops when it is done. Nothing loops while the page is idle.
  • Motion shows a real change. It never fakes activity.
  • Between pages: one continuous move. No fade-outs and no cuts.
  • Respect "reduce motion".

Copy

  • Name the reader first. Answer their first question first.
  • Say what happens, not how it should feel.
  • One idea per sentence.
  • No machine sentence shapes. docs/COPY-LAWS.md lists them.
  • Never invent proof.
  • A refusal names what failed, what is missing, and how to fix it.

Color

  • Color means something. Everything else is neutral.
  • One accent color is for the main action only.
  • Each chain kind has its own quiet color (section 4).
  • Colors are tokens. A new brand changes the token values, never the pages.

The home redesign

The mockup in this folder is one app with four views. The 3D world stays on screen and moves between them.

  1. Home. The network as rings around the FXN hub. Your own chain is one ring. A rail on the right lists the 13 capabilities and their checked cases.
  2. Your chain. Your ring rises. A page opens out of your tile and lists your records.
  3. A record. The page moves to one record: its text, who signed it, the versions before it, and the next record.
  4. Open record. The tile grows into a card with tabs: details, connections and history.
