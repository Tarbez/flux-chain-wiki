DEFXN · identity card · audit and plan

True site skinning, held by your identity

Written for: the owner, the defxn site session, the genesis session and the Ark UI owner. Status: 2026-10-24, by the NFT session, reviewed by the site session. Decisions 1 and 2 taken by the owner the same day. Every card here is a sample. No real card exists yet. The same text is in docs/home-redesign/nft/SKIN-AUDIT.md.

The answer first

Today a sample card dresses the defxn site from end to end: colours, chrome on 38 Ark UI parts, the account avatar, the profile card, and the Skin lab. A visitor wears it only by a review address or the profile switch, and nothing is kept.

Missing are a real card held by an identity, a consent step, a kept choice, a check that the site is genuine, layouts for the navbar, new kinds of element, and motion timelines.

Three moves make it real:

  1. The skin travels as a small signed package held by the identity: tokens, six drawings and rules. It is about 2 KB compressed. A site needs no NFT code to wear it.
  2. The identity decides where the skin goes. The broker hands the package only to a site whose name record verifies, and only after the person says yes.
  3. The trusted parts, the identity bar and the site's seal, are drawn outside any skin. A skin can never forge them, and a phishing copy cannot show them.

The experience we aim for

  1. You open a DEFXN site, for example radio.fxn. It shows its own look. At the end of its navbar sits your identity bar, drawn by your Ark browser, or on the plain web by the broker in its own frame. It names the site as its name record proves it: the name, verified, and who published this build.
  2. You press your card. Three choices appear: wear my card here, always wear it on verified DEFXN sites, or wear my look without saying who I am.
  3. You say yes. The broker hands the site your signed skin package. The site checks the signature with the one shared verifier.
  4. The site transforms once. Its colours change and its parts take the card's chrome. The navbar takes your card's arrangement once layouts are on. Your avatar appears. Your card's arrival timeline plays once and stops. The ring world stays as it is.
  5. The content stays the site's. You change how it looks, never where its links go.
  6. On a copy at a look-alike address there is no DEFXN name record. The identity bar says so, the broker refuses to sign you in or hand over your card, and your card never appears. The refusal is the protection. The missing card is the warning you can see.
  7. "Use the site's look" puts the site back exactly as it was.

The skin travels as a package, not as code

A skin is data the NFT engine derives once. A site needs only Ark UI's skin layer and the package.

  • Derived, never authored. Anyone can derive the package again from the record line and the public layer table. A site checks the identity's signature, and any DEFXN node can re-derive the package to audit it.
  • Drawings go by address. Each chrome drawing already has a content hash, so a package can name six hashes and every site shares one cache.
  • Stills replace the engine wherever 3D is not the content: about 55 KB of picture instead of about 213 KB of engine, and nothing to paint.
  • Storage is FLX: one grammar of records, proposed label DFXNSKIN1, held by the identity next to the card.

Wearing from the identity

Today the broker shares a public key and a display name with a site only after the person says yes to that exact origin. The yes lasts for that tab and that identity, and it carries no card. KASSET1, a unique-object capability with one owner, one content pointer, mint, transfer and burn, is built on a branch and not yet committed.

  1. The card is a KASSET1 object. Its owner is the identity's key, and its pointer is the card record. Ownership comes with the capability.
  2. The worn skin is a public record by the owner, naming the package's content address. A new skin appends a record that supersedes the old one, which is never rewritten.
  3. Private choices go in the Vault: where to always wear, the nav preferences, and whether to say who you are.
  4. The broker's reply gains a card. After the yes it returns the card's id and the package address. The site checks the bytes against the address and the ownership with the one verifier.
  5. "My look only" returns the package alone, without the key or the name. A rare card is still recognisable, and the sheet says so.

The visitor's navbar, holding the site's data

  • The site's zone keeps the site's brand, routes and products, from the site's own data. The card may change the arrangement, the density and the chrome. It never changes a destination, a label or the reading order.
  • The identity zone holds the avatar, the name, the site's seal and the wear switch. It is a locked part that no skin reaches. The site session confirms it fits: the lock lives in the skin's own part map, so the header's size budget is untouched.

It needs three things: the owner's review of Ark UI's existing layouts on the gallery page, the site's table turning layout and density on part by part, and a small nav preference record kept by the identity in FLX. Every preference points at the site's own data, so it cannot add a destination.

Verification, and a self-certified verification grid

The aim: a visitor's own client proves which site it is on, and a page cannot fake that proof.

One working chain exists today, on the server:

host nameto.fxn nametosigned proof, one verifiertoblake3_cid targettobytes that hash to it

What is missing: no record binds a name to its site, because dense-wire has no site-key or allowed-origin target. The broker trusts any *.defxn.com origin by a pattern. No visitor sees a seal or a name-proof state anywhere. The audit also found three defects. The site session fixed the first: the CMS publish script called a retired check that threw on every publish. Two stay open: the site's copy of the record builders writes old record kinds, and the gateway's pinned genesis key is empty in this checkout, which needs your key.

What to build, in the order that protects people first

  1. Bind a name to its site: two new dense-wire targets, the site's public key and its allowed origins, returned by the one verifier. No new store, claim path or verifier.
  2. The broker gates by name. It answers an origin only when a verified record lists it. A phishing page then gets no signature, key, name or card, whatever it looks like. The protection does not depend on people noticing.
  3. The client checks what it shows. The Ark browser fetches by content address, checks the hash, keeps the proof with the page, and shows the result in its own address bar:
    name verified with its build and owner   build does not match   no DEFXN name
  4. The identity bar lives outside the page: browser chrome in the Ark browser, and the broker's own window for anything that signs on the plain web.
  5. A personal seal. The identity bar draws a seal from a secret in the person's Vault and the verified name, using an emblem, a corner and a palette from the person's own card. Each person learns their own seal per site, and a page cannot compute it. The seal is a cue. The refusal is the gate.
  6. The verification grid. Signed statements about names in the planned Attestations resolver: official site of, impersonates, reported as a scam. Each is signed and can be retracted. Each reader chooses whose keys count: by default the network's committees, the people you follow, and you.
  7. Look-alike names are refused at the one claim path when public claims open, by their confusable skeleton.
  8. Provenance, never a verdict. For scams and propaganda the client shows signed facts with their signers. It never shows a conclusion the system did not reach, and there is no hidden list.
  9. A skin can never forge trust. The seal and the identity zone are locked parts, and a site wears only a package whose card verifies.

New elements, and elements that become components

  • Map element kinds to part kinds, such as surface, list, control, rule and heading, so no per-part list is written.
  • New slots go through the same pipeline: a layer folder, the owner's review, then the shop. Candidates are a control shape, a loader that stops when loading ends, a pointer for non-text areas, and a badge shape.
  • Shaped by people: a piece made in the CMS 3D studio becomes an element file signed by its maker, goes through review, then the shop.
  • Ark UI opens its closed sets by an extension hook, so a new value is added once, with its invariant tests.

Timelines from the 3D studio

  • Tracks and keyframes. A track targets a card piece or a part kind and one property from a closed set: move, turn, scale, clip or the drawing of a line. A trigger starts it: wearing, arriving, hover, press or focus.
  • Rails. Every timeline ends. The site has no fades, so it moves and clips. Reduced motion jumps to the last frame. Nothing flashes more than three times a second, and no control's hit area moves while it can be pressed.
  • Player and storage. The browser's own animation engine plays it, and the 3D engine draws only while it runs. It is stored as FLX records, proposed label DFXNTL1, and can be sold as an element.
  • A component is an Ark part, the pieces that dress it, its rules and its timeline. For example, an Arcade button with a cut edge, a 120 ms press, and an emblem that pulses once on hover.

Across DEFXN, Dead Ark and CR3TV

  • defxn carries the shared capabilities and the core resolvers. Dead Ark and CR3TV are app-chains that sign with defxn's Identity resolver, so one identity can wear one card on all three.
  • The skin client moves into the SDK beside Ark UI's skin layer, and every product syncs it. A site's part policy is already FLX, so its signed manifest can carry it, and no site copies another's table.
  • Dead Ark: the Ark browser is the trusted surface for the identity bar, the seal and the name-proof state.
  • CR3TV: a creator's channel wears the creator's card, and an official-channel mark becomes a signed attestation.

Why people wear, and buy

A worn card is seen by its wearer on every verified site, and by others wherever a profile shows. Every element is a shop item with a price derived from its odds. A site can say what a visitor wears, and one tap opens the shop at that element. Buying needs a verified balance source, which is not connected yet. Once live, measure from signed records only: how many visitors wear a card, how many wearers open the shop, and how many shop visits buy.

Decisions for the owner

  1. What is a real card?
    • DecidedA KASSET1 object. Its id is the mint record's address, its owner is the identity's key, and its pointer is the card record. Ownership, transfer and burning come with it.
  2. Does the ring world take the card's colours?
    • DecidedNever. The ring world stays as you made it, and the card shows in the chrome, the avatar and the profile.The site session makes the one change in the ring world's code.
  3. Where do layouts start?
    • My pickThe mega dropdown and tabs first, for cards that wear a whole set.Those parts have designed layouts already, and a set keeps them coherent.
    • Every card at once.
  4. Offer "my look only"?
    • My pickYes, with the note that a rare card is still recognisable.
    • No: wearing always says who you are.
  5. Whose attestations count by default?
    • My pickThe network's committees, the people you follow, and you.
    • Only the people you follow.
  6. The card's brand fonts on the site
    • My pickThe site's own fonts, keeping three font slots.
    • The card's own two fonts, hosted by the site.

The rules this plan keeps

  • Stored state is FLX. The package, the timelines and the choices are FLX records, never JSON.
  • One name system. Every proof goes through dense-wire's one verifier, with no new store or claim path.
  • Derive, do not author. Packages, rules and prices come from the layer table and the drawings.
  • Nothing idles. Every timeline ends, and the 3D engine draws only on demand.
  • A refusal names the failure, the missing proof and the remedy.
  • The client never shows a conclusion the system did not reach.
  • The ring world, the grid's core 3D, stays as the owner made it.
